starfall lite - ronan’s homebrew guide
yo welcome to starfall lite, the streamlined version of the combat system that actually gets used instead of sitting there looking pretty while you have one fight per year.
this is built for fast, narrative-focused combat that works for action rp without needing a grid, miniatures, or a degree in mathematics. if you can count to 5 and roll some dice, you're golden.
---
the core idea
starfall lite uses a dice pool system inspired by games like blades in the dark. instead of rolling d20s and adding modifiers, you roll multiple d6s and look at your HIGHEST die. more dice = better odds of success.
the whole system is designed around one question: "does this make the game more fun or does it make me do more bookkeeping?" if it's the latter, we cut it.
---
stats
we're using standard d&d stats cause they work for literally everything, not just combat:
str (strength) - melee attacks, grappling, heavy hits, smashing things, athletics
dex (dexterity) - ranged attacks, dodging, finesse weapons, acrobatics, stealth, initiative
con (constitution) - resisting poison/stun/exhaustion, endurance, not dying
int (intelligence) - studied magic (wizard style), tactics, investigation, knowing things
wis (wisdom) - intuitive magic (druid/cleric style), perception, reading people, survival
cha (charisma) - innate magic (sorcerer style), persuasion, intimidation, force of personality
each stat is rated 0-4 (not ability scores, just the rating). when you roll an attack, you roll that many d6s.
magic note: depending on how your character does magic, you use different stats. studied it in books? int. channel spirits or nature? wis. born with it or raw talent? cha. pick what fits your character's vibe.
---
dice pools - how rolling works
every time you make an attack roll, you roll a pool of d6s. you only look at the highest die to determine success. saves use d20s, not d6s - see the saves section for that.
rolling dice:
your base dice = your stat rating (minimum 1 die even at stat 0)
modifiers add or subtract dice from your pool
you always roll at least 1 die even if modifiers push you negative
examples:
3 str, no modifiers = roll 3d6
3 str, +1 attack bonus = roll 4d6
3 str, -2 from disadvantage = roll 1d6 (minimum)
advantage and disadvantage:
advantage and disadvantage are stackable dice modifiers. each stack of advantage adds 1 die to your pool. each stack of disadvantage removes 1 die from your pool. they cancel out net against each other.
+1 advantage on a 3d6 roll = 4d6
+2 advantage on a 3d6 roll = 5d6
-1 disadvantage on a 3d6 roll = 2d6
+2 advantage and -1 disadvantage on a 3d6 roll = net +1, so 4d6
you always look at just the single highest die regardless of pool size.
attack rolls: when you attack, roll dice based on the stat you're using:
melee attack with str 3 = roll 3d6
ranged attack with dex 4 = roll 4d6
magic blast with int 2 = roll 2d6
look at your highest die and compare it to the target's AC.
thresholds: AC uses three tiers:
tier 1 (easy)
highest die 4-6: clean success
highest die 3: success with a cost
highest die 1-2: failure
tier 2 (medium)
highest die 5-6: clean success
highest die 4: success with a cost
highest die 1-3: failure
tier 3 (hard)
highest die 6: clean success
highest die 5: success with a cost
highest die 1-4: failure
what "success with a cost" means: you hit, but something goes sideways. keep it simple and immediate. good costs:
you leave yourself open (attacker gets +1 advantage on their next attack against you)
you burn extra resources (costs 1 additional star or a few extra mp on top of the normal cost, etc)
partial effect (for multihit moves, one fewer hit lands)
minor chip (you take a small amount of damage from the exchange)
the gm picks whichever cost makes sense for the moment. don't overthink it.
critical success: if you roll multiple 6s in your dice pool, that's a crit.
on attacks: double your damage
on saves: you succeed AND gain advantage on your next roll
more than two 6s: additional benefit(s) (dm decision)
roll modifiers: effects and abilities can modify your dice pools in three ways:
attack modifier: adds or subtracts dice when YOU attack
+1 = roll 1 extra die
-1 = roll 1 fewer die
example: "haste" gives you +1 attack modifier, so you roll 1 more die on attacks
save modifier: adds or subtracts dice when YOU make saves (note: saves use d20, not d6. this modifier still adds or subtracts dice from the d20 roll's bonus - see saves section)
incoming modifier: affects attacks AGAINST you (enemies roll more or fewer dice)
example: "tempest fang hits you twice, giving you -2 incoming modifier"
next time someone attacks you, they roll 2 fewer dice
this represents you being harder to hit due to positioning, buffs, or debuffs on attackers
modifiers stack numerically:
you have +2 from haste and -1 from exhaustion = net +1
you have -2 incoming modifier from tempest fang and -1 from another effect = -3 total, attackers roll 3 fewer dice
---
the whole system is built around "more dice = better odds" which makes bonuses and penalties feel impactful without needing to do complex math.
---
powerscaling (yuck)
we don't do exp and levelling here cause it's for one-shot campaigns where people are at a set power level. characters come in three power levels based on their proficiency bonus:
rookie (prof +2):
total stat points: 10-14
hp pools: about 70% of standard ranges
new adventurers, trainees, people just starting out
veteran (prof +3):
total stat points: 12-16
hp pools: standard ranges
experienced fighters, most player characters
elite (prof +4):
total stat points: 16-20
hp pools: about 130% of standard ranges
legendary warriors, major bosses, peak performance
grunts (any prof):
hp: 50% of normal
damage: 50% of normal
meant to die in one good combo
disposable enemies, minions, fodder
when making a character, you distribute your stat points (0-4 in each stat, total based on tier). all stats start at 0, you just add points where you want them.
---
the action economy - 5 stars
every turn you get 5 stars to spend. stars refresh automatically at the start of your turn.
attacks cost stars:
light attack: 1 star
medium attack: 2 stars
heavy attack: 4-5 stars (varies by move, check the sheet)
utility: varies by move (check the sheet)
dash (movement): 1 star
the combo rule: if you hit, you can keep attacking (as long as you have stars). if you miss, your combo breaks and you can't attack anymore that turn. you can still use utility moves, transformations, or other non-attack actions.
this creates a risk/reward thing where you gotta decide: do i throw three light attacks and hope they all land, or do i go for one heavy and save stars in case i miss?
---
combat flow
here's how a typical fight goes:
initiative - everyone rolls 1d20 + dex modifier. highest goes first. turn order stays the same throughout combat.
your turn starts - you get 5 stars. any effects on you tick down by 1 round.
take actions - spend your stars on attacks, movement, abilities. remember: if you miss an attack, your combo breaks and you can't attack anymore (but you can still use non-attack stuff).
resolve reactions - if you have reaction abilities or someone wants to defend against your attack, handle that.
end turn - announce you're done. effects that last "until end of turn" expire.
next person goes - repeat until combat ends.
rounds: a round is when everyone has taken one turn. use this for effect durations.
---
deployables
some moves create deployables - things like clones, turrets, summons, pets, whatever.
how they work:
act on YOUR turn (not separate initiative)
have their own hp and star pool
have limited movesets (usually 2-4 moves max)
have duration (rounds or until destroyed)
auto-removed when duration ends or hp hits 0
example: "shadow clone (utility, 2 stars, 15 mp) - create clone with 20 hp, 3 stars per turn, knows tempest fang and thunderlash, lasts 3 rounds"
on your turn, you control both yourself and your deployables. keep their actions simple so it doesn't slow down combat.
---
final notes
this guide covers everything you need to run starfall lite. if you have questions that aren't answered here, the answer is probably "do whatever makes sense for your table."
remember: the system serves the story, not the other way around. if a rule gets in the way of fun, change it. if a rule makes things more fun, keep it.

saves
---
saving throws
sometimes you need to resist effects. that's what saves are for.
rolling saves: roll 1d20 + stat modifier + proficiency
nat 20: critical success, add some kinda benefit
meet or beat DC: success
below DC: failure
save types:
str - resisting being moved, breaking free
dex - dodging aoe, avoiding traps
con - resisting poison, enduring
int - resisting illusions, mind effects
wis - resisting fear, noticing things
cha - resisting banishment, force of will
spell save DC: if someone tries to resist your magical ability, the DC is:
DC = 8 + proficiency + highest mental stat (int/wis/cha)
---
skill checks
for non-combat stuff, roll 1d20 + stat + proficiency (if you're proficient in that skill).
skills by stat:
str: athletics
dex: acrobatics, sleight of hand, stealth
int: arcana, history, investigation, nature, religion
wis: animal handling, insight, medicine, perception, survival
cha: deception, intimidation, performance, persuasion
characters are proficient in 2-4 skills depending on their background. pick what makes sense.

resources n dmg
---
healing and resources
healing: effects that restore hp are straightforward. you can't heal above max hp (but temp hp can go over).
mp regeneration: mp doesn't regenerate automatically in combat. you need abilities, items, or rest to restore it. out of combat, mp fully restores on short rest (10 minutes).
temp hp: temporary hit points absorb damage before your real hp. temp hp doesn't stack - if you gain new temp hp, it replaces the old amount unless the new amount is lower. temp hp can exceed your max hp.
stars: refresh to 5 at the start of each of your turns automatically. some abilities might grant extra stars or cost stars, but by default you always start your turn with 5.
---
damage types and resistances
damage comes in different flavors: physical (slashing/piercing/bludgeoning), fire, cold, lightning, poison, necrotic, radiant, force, psychic.
resistance: take half damage from that type (round down)
vulnerability: take double damage from that type
immunity: take no damage from that type
these are character properties. a fire mage might have fire immunity and cold vulnerability, for example.
when you take damage, apply resistance/vulnerability before temp hp.
---
damage - flat and fast
no damage dice. every attack has a flat base damage plus your stat modifier.
damage ranges by attack type:
light: 4-6 base damage
medium: 8-12 base damage
heavy: 14-20 base damage
when you design a move, you pick a base damage in that range. then when you use it, you add your relevant stat rating.
example: tempest fang is a light attack with 4 base damage. if your dex is 3, you deal 4 + 3 = 7 damage total.
multihit attacks: some moves hit multiple times. each hit rolls separately. this means a multihit move CAN land all hits, but it's not guaranteed.
clean hit (all hits roll as hits): full damage
hit with cost: gm calls a cost (one fewer hit, or you take chip damage, etc.)
miss on a hit roll: that individual hit doesn't land. if your first roll in the combo misses, combo breaks.
the bot handles each roll separately and shows the total in one embed.
example: tempest fang hits twice. you roll the first hit, it lands. you roll the second hit, it also lands. both deal 4 damage for 8 total. if the second roll missed, only the first hit's damage goes through and the combo breaks.

character creation
here's how you build a character:
pick your power tier - rookie, veteran, or elite? this determines your stat points and hp range.
pick your archetype - choose from the 18 below. this gives you hp/mp/ac ranges and defines your playstyle.
distribute stats - spread your stat points (based on tier) across str/dex/con/int/wis/cha. each stat can be 0-4. all stats start at 0, add where you want.
set hp/mp/ac - pick numbers within your archetype's ranges. you can go slightly outside if you have a good reason (weird transformation, unique backstory, whatever) but try to stay close.
set proficiency - +2 for rookie, +3 for veteran, +4 for elite. proficiency adds to skill checks and determines your spell save DC.
design 8-15 moves - more on this below, but you want a mix of light/medium/heavy attacks plus some utility. quality over quantity.
(optional) add forms - if your character transforms, create alternate forms with different stats and movesets. transformation costs vary per character and can pull from mp, hp, or stars. check the character sheet.

effects n power ups
---
status effects
the bot tracks effects by name and duration. that's it. you remember what they do.
common effects:
🔥 burning - taking fire damage each turn
🩸 bleeding - taking physical damage each turn
☠️ poisoned - taking poison damage each turn
💫 stunned - loses all stars next round, can't act or move
🔻 prone - melee attacks vs you have advantage
👁️ blinded - disadvantage on attacks
🐌 slowed - halved dex, half movement (narrative)
⛓️ restrained - can't move (narrative but works like a stun)
🎯 marked - next attack vs you has advantage
⬆️ advantage - next roll has advantage
⬇️ disadvantage - next roll has disadvantage
when you add an effect, you give it a name and duration. the bot counts down each round. you handle the actual mechanics.
no stacking, no compound effects, no complexity. if you want multiple effects, apply multiple simple effects. don't make "burning poison" that does both, just apply burning AND poisoned separately.
---
transformations
some characters can transform into stronger (or different) forms. transformations swap your stats and moveset.
basic transformation:
costs vary per character and can come from mp, stars, hp, or a combination. check the character sheet for specifics.
hp carries over (usually receives some kinda benefit to make up for it since max hp doesnt change)
mp carries over too
stats change completely to the new form's stats
moveset swaps to the new form's moves
ac can change too
designing forms: each form should feel meaningfully different. don't just make "character but +1 to everything." change the playstyle.
example:
base form: rushdown, lots of light attacks, speed focused
storm form: bruiser, heavy hits, tankier, different moves entirely
no move retention: you don't keep base form moves in transformed state. pick one set of moves or the other. keeps it clean.

philosophy n guidelines
---
design philosophy
the whole point of starfall lite is to maximize the fun parts of combat (making choices, landing hits, cool abilities) while minimizing the boring parts (tracking 12 different effects, calculating modifiers, looking up rules).
when in doubt, ask:
does this make combat faster or slower?
does this create interesting choices or just busywork?
will i remember how this works in two weeks?
does this feel cool when it happens?
if the answer to any of those is bad, simplify or cut it.
narrative over simulation: starfall lite trusts you to describe what's happening. the numbers exist to create tension and stakes, not to simulate physics. if something doesn't make sense numerically but makes perfect sense in the story, go with the story.
player agency: the system assumes you're playing with people you trust. there's no extensive rules lawyering or "gotcha" mechanics. if someone's being a dick about the rules, that's a people problem, not a rules problem.
---
designing moves - the fun part
moves are what make your character unique. here's how to make good ones:
move structure: every move needs:
name (what you call it)
type (light/medium/heavy/utility)
star cost (1 for light, 2 for medium, 4-5 for heavy, varies for utility)
mp cost (if any)
stat (what you roll with)
damage (base number before stat modifier)
hits (1 for single hit, more for multihit - each hit rolls separately)
effect (what special thing it does, if anything)
cooldown or uses (if limited)
good move design: the best moves are simple, immediate, and don't require tracking a bunch of state across turns.
good triggers: on hit, on crit, on miss, when you use this, instant
good costs: spend hp, spend mp, spend stars
good benefits: positioning (knockback, teleport), resource trades (damage for healing), conditional bonuses, status effects
bad move design: avoid anything that makes you go "wait, how does this work again?" after you haven't used it for a week.
bad triggers: stacking counters, multi-phase activation, "the third time you do X"
bad effects: compound status effects, things that interact with other effects, mechanics that need a spreadsheet
the categories:
positioning moves - movement and placement. example: "whirlwind knee (medium, 10 damage) - on hit, knock target back 15ft and they must make a dex save or fall prone"
resource trades - spend one thing, get another. example: "blood strike (heavy, 16 damage) - spend 10 hp to deal an extra 8 damage"
conditional bonuses - if X then Y. example: "executioner (heavy, 18 damage) - if target is below 50% hp, deal double damage"
status application - inflict conditions. example: "stunning blow (medium, 10 damage) - on hit, target is stunned for 1 round"
multihit attacks - hit multiple times, each roll separate. example: "tempest fang (light, 4 damage, 2 hits) - each hit gives attackers disadvantage until your next turn"
defensive triggers - react to being hit. example: "counter slash (reaction, 6 damage) - when an enemy misses you with a melee attack, make a light attack"
setup moves - create lasting effects. example: "shadow clone (utility, 2 stars, 15 mp) - create a clone with 20 hp that copies your light attacks for 3 rounds"
combo extensions - keep hitting after missing. example: "relentless assault (passive) - once per turn, if you miss, you can continue your combo anyway"
risk/reward - gamble for power. example: "reckless strike (heavy, 20 damage) - attack with disadvantage, but crits deal triple damage instead of double"
situational utility - only works in specific contexts. example: "aerial ace (medium, 12 damage) - can only be used while airborne, never misses"
example character moves:
hin'obi (crowd controller, duelist archetype):
tempest fang (light, 1 star, 4 damage, 2 hits) - each hit gives attackers -1 incoming modifier until hin's next turn (stacks)
thunderlash (medium, 2 stars, 10mp, 8+int damage) - hit targets are marked, take +3 from all attacks for 2 rounds
cloud formation (utility, 2 stars, 10 mp) - gain advantage on attacks for 2 rounds
licér (medium, 2 stars, 12mp, 12+int damage) - concentrated lightning bolt
licér wi rós (heavy, 4 stars, 18mp) - raging typhoon, targets make dex save each round or take 5+int damage, disadvantage on attacks while in it
---
what to avoid
things we specifically DON'T do in starfall lite:
❌ damage dice - flat damage only
❌ opportunity attacks - YESN’T….. no combat map, positioning is narrative so we do them SOMETIMES
❌ compound status effects - burning poison? no. burning AND poisoned? yes.
❌ bonus actions - everything costs stars, keep it unified
❌ leveling up - this isn't a campaign system, it's for pvp and one-shots
❌ experience points - not relevant
❌ multiclassing - not a thing here
❌ feats - build it into your moves instead
